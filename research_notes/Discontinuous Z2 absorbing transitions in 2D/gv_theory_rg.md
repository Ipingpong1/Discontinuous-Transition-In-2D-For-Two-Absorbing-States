# Theory of Z2-symmetric two-absorbing-state transitions (Langevin, field theory/RG, mean-field/pair approximations): what is established about first order in d = 2

Scope note. "Read" levels below: FULL = full text read or searched (arXiv version unless stated); PARTIAL = relevant sections searched in full text; ABSTRACT = abstract/metadata only. Relevance: High/Medium/Low for the D&N claim (a fully first-order Z2-absorbing transition in 2D, and a first-order line ending at the 2D voter point). "Threat" = whether the source undermines the novelty claim. Search date 2026-10-27. Full texts used were downloaded from arXiv; project-local copies in `campaign/theory/lit/` were re-read for exact quotes.

Terminology warning that runs through every section. In this literature "first-order" or "discontinuous" is often used for the voter/GV point itself because the magnetization jumps from 0 to ±1 (β = 0), even though the interface density vanishes continuously and the point is critical. Examples: Dornic et al. 2001 ("first-order d = 2 nonequilibrium critical point"), Ódor's RMP ("They exhibit first order transition still dynamical scaling can be observed"), Canet et al. 2005 ("direct, first-order-like GV transition"), Dickman & Tretyakov 1995, Krause et al. 2012 ("abrupt"), Rodrigues et al. 2015 and Llabrés et al. 2026 ("GV transition line A = B/3" in mean field). Al Hammal et al. coined "fully first-order" (the interface density also jumps) to separate the two. The D&N claim must be phrased in that second, stronger sense and should also cite the coexistence/nucleation evidence, or a referee can say "GV is already first order".

## Q1. Al Hammal, Chaté, Dornic & Muñoz (2005): what was integrated, what is claimed, and what "d = 2 is the upper critical dimension" does and does not imply

### Takeaway
Al Hammal et al. did not rule out a first-order transition in 2D. They integrated their Langevin equation at a single noise setting (D = 0.5, σ² = 0.8) with b no lower than about −2. Their 2D GV claim for b < b* rests on one log-decay plot (b = −0.2) and an interface-density check that is "not shown". Their only theoretical input for b < 0 is a mean-field "we expect" plus a consistency remark that d = 2 is the GV upper critical dimension. Nothing in the paper is an RG or rigorous statement. The "d = 2 = d_c" remark concerns the voter fixed point (marginal interfacial noise). It does not forbid a first-order line in strongly bistable or weak-noise regimes. In mean field, b < 0 is itself a first-order (Maxwell) transition.

### Inferences
- The user's suspicion is correct. Al Hammal et al. did not exclude first order in 2D; they did not observe it at one noise setting. Every theoretical element is mean-field ("we expect") or a dimensional-consistency remark; no 2D RG exists in the paper. The interface-density continuity at a_GV for b < 0 is "not shown", and log-decay evidence is shown only at b = −0.2.
- **Strength of bistability actually probed (my calculation, not in the papers).** With V(φ) above, V(±1) − V(0) = −a/4 + b/12, so the mean-field Maxwell (equal-depth) line for b < 0 is a = b/3 and the mean-field spinodal of the active state is a = 0. Llabrés et al. 2026 independently state "the GV transition line A = B/3" in mean field ([arXiv:2505.11358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11358)).
  - Al Hammal's most negative point is b = −2, a_GV ≈ −1. There the barrier between φ = 0 and ±1 is V_max ≈ 0.10, against a local noise variance σ²/2 = 0.4 at φ = 0.
  - Azizi & Pleimling at b = −8, a = −4.132: barrier ≈ 0.44 against σ²/2 = 1.
  - In both cases the 2D transition lies deep inside the region where mean field says the active state is globally stable (a < b/3), and the per-site barrier is below the per-site noise.
  - So the published "GV for b < 0" evidence is all at strong noise relative to the deterministic bistability.
- **Dimensional analysis (my calculation).** Rescaling t and x in Eq. (2) in d = 2 leaves two dimensionless parameters, a/b and g = σ²/D, plus a lattice cutoff ratio Δx·√(|b|/D). Making b more negative at fixed σ² and D mostly moves the cutoff; it does not weaken the continuum noise. The values are g = 1.6 for Al Hammal, 0.5 for Dornic–Chaté–Muñoz's voter test, and 16 for Azizi–Pleimling (D = h/4 = 0.125, σ² = 2). The weak-noise, strongly bistable corner of the GV equation (g ≪ 1, b < 0) has never been integrated in 2D.
- **What "d = 2 = d_c(GV)" implies.** It is a statement about the voter fixed point. The multiplicative interfacial-noise vertex is marginal at d = 2 (Janssen's exact CDP RG: d_c = 2, log flow; Q2). It explains the 1/ln t decay and the β = 0 m-jump at GV points that flow to the voter fixed point. In d ≥ 3 the voter model has an active stationary state (ρ > 0), so crossing a voter-like point gives a ρ-jump with no nucleation barrier needed. That is plausibly what "fully first-order" in d = 3 reflects.
- **What it does not imply.** It is not a theorem that every 2D Z2-absorbing transition flows to the GV point. Transitions far from the voter point, or with strong bistability, can be controlled by a discontinuity (first-order) fixed point, as in equilibrium Blume–Capel-type diagrams. Mean field (which applies above d_c) already says the b < 0 transition is first order (Maxwell a = b/3). The 2D GV result for b < 0 is a fluctuation effect established only numerically and only at strong noise.
- The 3D "fully first-order" claim was "preliminary". I found no later publication with the 3D Langevin data. Lipowski & Droz 2002 measured β = 1 (continuous ρ) in a 3D q = 2 absorbing Potts model, later reinterpreted as an Ising+DP split (Q6). So even the 3D claim is not firmly established in microscopic models.

## Q2. RG and field theory of the GV/DP2/PC class and the voter-model Langevin equation: is there any RG statement about first order in d = 2?

### Takeaway
No RG or field-theory result addresses, let alone excludes, a first-order Z2-absorbing transition in d = 2. The available RG covers three things:
- the BARW/PC particle problem (Cardy–Täuber), whose d = 2 transition sits at zero branching and is not the spin problem in 2D;
- the d = 1 PC/GV fixed point via NPRG at μ = 0, i.e. with no bistable cubic term (Canet et al.);
- the voter/CDP point and a linear bias, solved exactly with d_c = 2 and logarithmic flows (Janssen), plus numerical checks of the voter Langevin equation (Dornic–Chaté–Muñoz).

No tricritical or first-order fixed point and no runaway-flow analysis for Z2-absorbing problems was found. Janssen's exact d = 2 RG does say that near the voter point the noise coupling is marginally irrelevant and reaction terms are log-renormalized. That bears directly on a first-order line ending at the voter point (Q4).

### Inferences
- No RG, perturbative or NPRG, has been carried out for the GV Langevin equation with the bistable term (μ > 0 / b < 0) in any dimension, nor for d = 2 with a and b both non-zero. So there is no RG basis for "no first order in 2D". Equally, no first-order or tricritical GV fixed point has been computed.
- **Synthesis (my inference from Janssen plus the Cox–Perkins coalescing-walk picture, Q4).** At the 2D voter fixed point the field n is dimensionless, so any reaction polynomial ñ·F(n) with F(0) = F(1) = 0 has naive coupling dimension 2. Each extra insertion of the interface field ϕ = n(1 − n) carries an extra logarithmic factor X(l).
  - The leading Z2-odd reaction term ñ ϕ(1 − 2n) is a cubic, which cannot be bistable.
  - The bistability-generating terms, e.g. ñ ϕ²(1 − 2n), are suppressed by further 1/ln factors.
  - This is the field-theory counterpart of the 3-class versus 4+-class coalescing-walk hierarchy, q3 ~ K/(log t)³, used in the project's VPT.
  - It predicts a first-order window near the 2D voter point that closes only logarithmically. That supports the project's "first-order line ending at the voter point as a marginal point" picture, but it has not been computed in the literature.
- The NPRG F* (Canet) lives at d < 4/3 and μ = 0. Its relation to 2D Z2 spin models is nil, beyond establishing that the PC/GV fixed point is non-perturbative in d = 1.

## Q3. Microscopic-to-Langevin mappings and the "washout" argument (Vazquez & López 2008; Russell & Blythe 2011; Castellano, Muñoz & Pastor-Satorras 2009)

### Takeaway
Vazquez & López map flip probabilities to Al Hammal's (a, b) through low-order derivatives of the flip function at zero local field. This is a mean-field-level, smooth-rate expansion. They never analyse the b < 0 coexistence regime and make no first-order prediction. Their 2D simulations found only GV for z = 4 and 8. The "washout" argument of Castellano et al. is a zero-dimensional, one-field argument. It was tested in 2D on one weakly bistable model at one point (q = 4, ε = 1/4) plus "data not shown", so it is not a general theorem. Russell & Blythe show that in discrete-space models the noise strength itself can switch surface-tension (Ising) ordering to voter ordering at a finite threshold. That means noise level, not just symmetry, can decide between voter-like and surface-tension-driven behaviour.

- **Washout argument: assumptions and exact scope.**
  1. One-component field. The active phase is the origin φ ≈ 0 of a single magnetization variable, with no separate activity or interface field.
  2. Multiplicative noise of O(1) at the origin, vanishing at the barriers.
  3. Zero-dimensional reasoning about the asymmetry of escape. Front motion, surface tension of active/absorbing interfaces and nucleation barriers are not analysed.
  4. Tested numerically on one weakly bistable model point. From their eq. (14), with φ = 2x − 1, I find (my algebra) a = (3 − 14ε)/8 and b = −(1 − 2ε)/8 in Al Hammal's form.   It is therefore not evidence against first order in strongly bistable rules, nor in models whose active phase is not "the origin of φ".

Conversely, a lattice rule whose active phase is self-stabilising (D&N, single spins, z = 8) can show first-order phenomenology only through mechanisms beyond the one-field GV potential picture, such as correlated local configurations acting as a second, fast "activity" field. This is consistent with the project's PA finding that ρ is a fast variable with its own stable branch.
- The z = 8 coincidence matters. Vazquez–López (polynomial rules) and Droz et al. 2003 (absorbing Potts) both found GV-only behaviour at z = 8. D&N, also at z = 8, claims first order. This sharpens the novelty but also invites a referee to suspect a long transient. The Lipowski–Droz history in Q6 is a precedent.

## Q4. General arguments for or against first-order Z2-absorbing transitions in 2D, and how the marginal 2D voter point interacts with bistable perturbations (including a first-order line ending at the voter point)

### Takeaway
No general no-go exists for clean 2D Z2-absorbing systems.
- Hinrichsen's no-go is explicitly 1D.
- The Imry–Ma-type no-go (Villa Martín et al.) requires quenched disorder, and even that has a claimed 2D counterexample.
- First-order absorbing transitions with tricritical endpoints are well established in clean 2D single-absorbing-state models.

The strongest relevant rigorous results come from voter-model perturbation theory.
- In d ≥ 3, Cox–Durrett–Perkins prove coexistence in a bistable regime next to the voter point (Case 4A: the absorbing state is linearly stable, yet the active phase wins). They leave the mirror case open.
- In d = 2, the reaction-function machinery is unavailable. Cox–Perkins use the 3-class drift Θ3, a cubic that cannot be bistable.
- Cox–Merle–Perkins prove that a d ≥ 3 wedge-shaped phase region becomes a "thorn" at the 2D voter point, with width/distance ~ 1/log².

Together these support, but do not prove, the scenario of a first-order line that ends at the 2D voter point with a window closing logarithmically.

### Takeaway
I swept forward citations of Al Hammal 2005, Canet 2005, Vazquez–López 2008 and Castellano 2009, plus Dornic 2001, Droz 2003, Lipowski–Droz 2002, Russell–Blythe 2011, Castellano–Pastor-Satorras 2012, Rodrigues 2015, Azizi–Pleimling 2020 and Llabrés 2026, and ran an arXiv full-catalogue query. No paper claims a fully first-order (interface-density jump, metastability, nucleation) transition in a 2D Z2-symmetric two-absorbing-state model. The closest prior items are:
1. Rodrigues et al. 2015: a "discontinuous" P–A line ending at the voter point on a 2D triangular lattice, but by the magnetization-jump criterion, drawn from mean field, and contradicted by Azizi–Pleimling 2020's GV line.
2. Lipowski & Droz 2002: d = q = 2 "inconclusive", leaning first order from ρ(t) data, but later reassigned to the voter class by Droz et al. 2003. This is a cautionary precedent.
3. Llabrés et al. 2026: a discontinuous "Modified GV" transition and tricritical point, only once noise removes the absorbing states, and mean-field or networks only.

### Inferences
- **Novelty.** The claim "a fully first-order (ρ-jump, with metastability, critical droplets and nucleation-limited spreading) transition in a 2D model with two Z2-symmetric absorbing states" appears novel as of Oct 2026. Four items must be cited and distinguished:
  1. Rodrigues 2015. Mean-field-derived line, m-jump criterion only, small L, disputed by Azizi–Pleimling.
  2. Lipowski–Droz 2002. A suggestive but inconclusive ρ "jump" that turned out voter-like; the project should cite it pre-emptively and show why D&N differs (L-independent lifetimes, front-velocity sign change, droplets).
  3. Al Hammal 2005. 3D "fully first-order", 2D GV at strong noise only.
  4. Llabrés 2026. MGV and tricritical point only without absorbing states, mean-field or networks.
- **Positioning.** The D&N result directly contradicts the Dornic conjecture as extended by Al Hammal, Castellano and Azizi–Pleimling, that the generic direct transition in 2D is GV. It does so at z = 8, where two earlier microscopic families found GV only. This is the strongest selling point and also the point that will draw the most scrutiny.
- **The "first-order line ends at the voter point" part** has the Lipowski–Droz (q, d) = (2, 2) crossing-point conjecture as its closest physics precedent, and CMP's 2D thorn plus CDP's d ≥ 3 Case 4A as its closest rigorous analogues. No paper computes it.
